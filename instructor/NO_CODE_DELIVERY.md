# Delivery with zero programmers

This is the default route while participant experience is unknown. It builds on the participant handbook, capstone and runbook added in the curriculum revision. It replaces mandatory coding labs with supplied evidence, calculations, critique and decisions. No participant needs to install software or modify code. Python is an optional instructor demonstration and a technical extension.

## Start here

1. Print participant/NO_CODE_WORKBOOK.md for 20 people plus five spares, and the method card and capstone brief. Keep instructor/NO_CODE_ANSWER_KEY.md for the instructor only. The generated handbook includes the no-code workbook first.
2. Read the answers before teaching. Rehearse the calculation and one plain-language explanation for every activity.
3. Arrange five groups of four. Rotate decision owner, evidence checker, critical reviewer and spokesperson every activity. Each person writes an individual exit answer and final assessment.
4. Brief the Arabic interpreter using the existing draft glossary. Use short sentences, pause after one idea, and avoid speaking while participants read or calculate. Have the interpreter validate Arabic terminology; do not treat draft translations as approved.
5. Open saved results before class. Do not troubleshoot a live demo for more than one minute. Paper activities remain the main route even if the demonstration works.

## Choose the route from demonstrated comfort

At entry ask: What decisions do you make? Can you interpret a table? Can you explain an average and a percentage? Do you already use Python independently? Participants need not know a model-training library. Do not infer ability from nationality, title or English fluency.

If nobody or only a few people code, use this route for everyone, with optional notebook extensions for volunteers. If the group comfortably codes and wants implementation practice, use the original coding runbook. A mixed group can submit the same briefing from either supplied results or notebook output. Do not make another participant's programming ability a prerequisite for completing the course.

Common assessed outcomes: audit evidence, choose methods, design evaluation, interpret uncertainty, challenge claims and recommend an action. Coding-route additional outcome: implement and run the supplied workflow. The no-code route does not establish independent model-building competence. Share PROGRAMME.md with the organiser so that the expected depth is explicit.

## Days 1–4 · exactly 300 minutes including breaks

All timings include instructions and consecutive interpretation; they are not uninterrupted English speaking time. During labs, translate the written instruction once, then support groups as needed. Rehearse the pace with the interpreter.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Welcome/recap, individual entry or retrieval question, interpreted discussion |
| 20–50 | Concepts and worked example: around 15 minutes English plus 15 interpretation |
| 50–90 | Workbook A · 40 minutes |
| 90–105 | Debrief · around 7 minutes English plus interpretation |
| 105–120 | Break |
| 120–150 | Next concepts and example · around 15 minutes English plus interpretation |
| 150–195 | Workbook B · 45 minutes |
| 195–210 | Break |
| 210–260 | Workbook C and capstone · 50 minutes |
| 260–285 | Five groups report · five minutes each including interpretation |
| 285–300 | Individual exit answer, translated recap and parking-list question |

Three activity blocks total 135 minutes per day. Explain one calculation, then let groups perform and defend it. For slow groups, supply the computed number and assess its interpretation. Faster groups challenge assumptions or do a notebook extension. Preserve breaks, individual assessment and capstone work; shorten optional demonstrations first.

## Day 5 · exactly 300 minutes including breaks

Follow the same sequence through minute 210. At 210–225 finish capstone pages;225–260 present five groups, seven minutes each (three English, three interpretation, one feedback). At 260–280 administer the individual final practical from the workbook (five minutes translated instructions,15 writing). At 280–285 collect and discuss one key misconception. At 285–300 collect feedback and exit self-assessment and explain the post-course pack. Individual written capstones ensure all 20 participants contribute; do not attempt 20 interpreted presentations in 35 minutes.

## What to teach each day

| Day | Main decision | Before A | Before B | Protect in the debrief |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Can we trust the channel report? | Missing, duplicate, invalid and valid unusual records | Revenue, denominators and chart axes | Difference between a description and a causal budget decision |
| 2 | Is the model useful at our error costs? | Target, available features, split and baseline | Confusion matrix, precision, recall and cost | The preferred option can change when costs change |
| 3 | Can we trust this forecast and text classifier? | Time order, trend, seasonality, simple forecast and ARIMA | Text labels, ambiguity, language coverage and human review | Data availability at the forecast cutoff |
| 4 | What action follows from uncertainty? | Prior, observations, posterior and probability | Exact solution, MCMC propose/accept/stay, diagnostic limits | Parameter uncertainty differs from future outcomes |
| 5 | Is this ready for a monitored pilot? | Partitioned sums/counts; Hadoop storage/resource roles, MapReduce and Spark | Pipeline, held-out model results, neural model tradeoffs and GAN spread | Owner, monitoring, stop condition and rollback |

Use the existing handbook for concept explanations and the question bank for follow-ups. The new workbook is the activity source; original notebook labs are extensions, not additional compulsory work.

## Using the existing slides

The five generated PPTX files are drafts with visual placeholders. Canva designs are separate and have outstanding review issues. Neither has been rewritten by this adaptation. For current delivery use their conceptual slides selectively and display or print the workbook for activities. Preview every selected slide during rehearsal.

| Existing slide/lab prompt | Replacement instruction |
|---|---|
| Install, import or run a notebook | Open today's printed workbook; all required evidence is supplied |
| Day 1 clean the dataframe | Mark decisions on the eight-row worksheet; do not mix its totals with the 122-row demo |
| Day 2 fit/tune a model | Compare supplied errors and calculate error costs |
| Day 3 run ARIMA/vectorise text | Evaluate forecast errors and route ambiguous messages |
| Day 4 run MCMC/generate data | Audit supplied simulation evidence and apply the fictional decision policy |
| Day 5 run Spark/MLlib/GAN | Combine partition summaries and judge pilot readiness |

Do not announce a slide's coding deliverable as compulsory. Numerical slide examples may use the original notebook dataset; introduce worksheet cases explicitly as separate examples. If a draft slide is misleading or unfinished, use the corresponding handbook passage instead.

## One-week instructor preparation

| Prep day | Required rehearsal and tangible result |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read this guide, programme and capstone; collect participant diagnostic if available; confirm hours and interpretation mode |
| 2 | Solve Day 1 without the key, check answers, practise the briefing; print a sample worksheet |
| 3 | Solve Day 2 and rehearse explaining the confusion matrix and cost reversal |
| 4 | Solve Day 3; practise explaining chronological splits and ambiguous text |
| 5 | Solve Day 4; explain the difference between rate and count uncertainty aloud |
| 6 | Solve Day 5, complete a sample capstone and mark the final practical using the key |
| 7 | Rehearse a translated 30-minute segment, preview selected slides, print packs and save offline copies |

Run notebooks only for demonstrations you actually intend to show. If explanation of a technical extension is not secure, keep it optional and use the saved evidence. Record unanswered questions and verify them before replying. The instructor still needs to understand the results; eliminating mandatory code does not eliminate subject preparation.

## Remaining checks

Confirm whether five hours includes breaks: this route provides 270 contact minutes/day. If 25 contact hours are required, add 30 teaching minutes daily and place breaks outside those hours. Consecutive interpretation and group timing need rehearsal. A licensed real dataset and full slide polishing remain separate unfinished work. This route is hands-on decision practice with synthetic evidence, not a claim that every requested advanced implementation skill has been taught.
